So here’s the tool for this lecture. I call it the Build Check: five questions you answer before anyone writes code. One, Decision: what decision changes, and who acts on it? Two, Data: is the data actually there, on time, and good enough to trust? Three, Lightest path: what is the simplest integration pattern that would work? Four, Ownership: who owns this after go-live — the feed, the model and the alert? Five, Proof: how will we know it worked, and when would we turn it off? You score each question Go, Caution or Stop. I’ll show you how to turn those scores into a decision at the end. Two things about this check. It’s fast — for most requests you can do it in one working session with the clinical requester and someone from the interface team. And it’s written down. The output isn’t a feeling, it’s a one-page record you can show to anyone who asks why you built something, or why you didn’t. To make this concrete, I’ll run every question on the sepsis risk pipeline you’ll build in Track C.
