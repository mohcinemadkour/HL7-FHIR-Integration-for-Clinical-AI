SEPSIS PIPELINE

Rapid response nurse escalates earlier, at the vitals check.

Question one: what decision changes, and who acts on it? On the left are the four things you need to write down. First, the person who acts — a role, not “the care team.” Second, the moment in their workflow where they act. Third, how often that moment happens — every shift for every patient is very different from once a month. Fourth, the stakes. Rank the problem: patient safety first, then revenue and compliance, then convenience. Convenience is real, but on its own it rarely justifies a real-time integration. The Stop signal is the vague answer. If the best anyone can say is “clinicians will find it useful,” you don’t have a use case yet. Go back to the requester and ask: who, when, and what will they do differently? Here’s the sepsis pipeline. The person is the rapid response nurse. The moment is the routine vitals check. The decision is whether to escalate — call the physician, start the sepsis workup — earlier than they would have. It happens for many patients, every shift, and it’s a safety issue. That’s a clear Go. If you take one thing from this slide: a use case is a person, a moment and an action. If you can’t fill in all three, stop here.
