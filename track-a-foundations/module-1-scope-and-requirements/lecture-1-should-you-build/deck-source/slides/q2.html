SEPSIS PIPELINE · CAUTION

Vitals are timely. Lactate turnaround varies — model must handle a missing value.

Question two: is the data there, on time, and trustworthy? This is where ML engineers usually feel at home, but in a hospital the traps are different. Latency first. Your decision has a window. If the nurse decides at the vitals check, and the data you need arrives two hours later, the integration is useless — no matter how good the model is. Completeness. Don’t trust the data dictionary. Pull a real sample, de-identified, and measure how often the field is actually filled in. A field that exists in the schema and is empty for half the patients is a very different input. Coding. Are labs coded in LOINC, or in local codes that change from site to site? That decides how much mapping work — and mapping maintenance — you’re signing up for. And the source of truth. If the same value lives in three systems, pick one and write it down. The Stop signal: the data arrives after the decision is made. An interface can’t fix that, and neither can a better model. Garbage in, garbage out — and late in is just as bad. On the sepsis pipeline, I’d score this Caution. Vitals are charted on time. Lactate is the problem: turnaround varies, so at the moment of scoring it’s often missing. That’s not a Stop — it’s a requirement. The model has to handle a missing lactate, and you’ll do exactly that in Track C, when we build features from FHIR.
