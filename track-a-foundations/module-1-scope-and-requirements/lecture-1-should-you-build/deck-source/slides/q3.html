Each step up buys speed — and adds run cost. Climb only as high as the decision window needs.

Question three: what is the simplest pattern that would work? Engineers love to jump to the top of this ladder. Real-time, event-driven, inside the EHR — it’s the most interesting thing to build. It’s also the most expensive thing to run. So look at the ladder from the bottom. Step one: a manual step or a report. For something that happens a few times a month, a well-designed report or a checklist can deliver most of the value for almost none of the cost. Step two: a nightly batch, or a FHIR Bulk Data export. If the decision happens tomorrow morning, real-time buys you nothing. Step three: a FHIR query on demand — your app asks for the data when a user opens it. No feed to maintain. Step four: a real-time HL7 v2 feed through Mirth. Now you have a channel to monitor, messages to replay, and an upgrade to survive every year. Step five: a CDS Hook inside the EHR workflow. The highest impact and the highest cost — app registration, security review, alert design, clinician sign-off. The rule: climb only as high as the decision window from question one requires. And before you climb, ask whether a non-integration fix — a workflow change, or training — solves the real problem. For sepsis, the window is hours, and the action happens inside the EHR. So steps four and five are justified. That’s a Go — and in lecture 2 we’ll go deeper on choosing between these patterns.
