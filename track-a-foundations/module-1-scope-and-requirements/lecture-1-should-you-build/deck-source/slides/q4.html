SEPSIS PIPELINE · GO

Interface team: channel. ML owner: model. Nurse manager: alert.

Question four: who owns it after go-live? A clinical AI integration has at least three moving parts, and each one needs a named owner. The feed and the channel: usually the interface team. When an Epic upgrade changes a segment, they fix it. The model and its monitoring: that’s probably you, or your ML team. When performance drifts, you investigate and revalidate. The alert and the workflow: that needs a clinical owner — a nurse manager, a medical director. When nurses start ignoring the alert, someone on the clinical side has to care, and has the authority to change the workflow. And if there are vendors — the EHR, an integration engine, a third-party app — write down who is responsible for what, and get it into the contract before signing. When something breaks at a boundary between two vendors, you don’t want to discover then that each one thinks it’s the other’s job. The Stop signal: the owner is “the project team.” Projects end. Teams get reassigned. Pipelines keep running — until they quietly break and nobody notices. Three names. That’s a Go.
