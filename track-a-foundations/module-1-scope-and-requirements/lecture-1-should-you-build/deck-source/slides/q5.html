Question five: how will we know it worked? This is the question I care about most, because evaluation is what I do every day at EvalTrust — and it’s the one teams skip most often. You need four things, written down before you build. One primary metric, tied directly to the decision from question one. Not model AUC — that tells you the model ranks patients well, not that anything changed for them. The metric is about the action: did the nurse escalate sooner? A baseline, measured before go-live. Without it, you can’t show improvement, and you can’t defend the project when the budget review comes. Guardrails: the things that tell you it’s doing harm. For alerts, that’s the false-alert rate and the override rate. If nurses override almost every alert, you’re creating alert fatigue, and you’re making things worse. And kill criteria with a review date: “if the primary metric hasn’t moved by this date, or overrides stay above this level, we turn it off.” Deciding that in advance is what makes it a real decision. The Stop signal: “we’ll figure out how to measure it after launch.” You won’t — and by then nobody will want to. For sepsis: the primary metric is time from first warning signs to escalation. The guardrail is the alert override rate. And we review at [__] days. That’s a Go.
