For clinical AI, CDS Hooks is the pattern that matters most, because it’s where your model meets a human decision. So let’s walk through how it works. Step one: a clinician does something in the EHR — opens a patient’s chart, or starts signing an order. Those moments are called hooks; patient-view and order-sign are two common ones. Step two: the EHR calls your CDS service over HTTPS. It can send prefetched FHIR data with the call — recent vitals, labs — so your service doesn’t have to query for them. Step three: your model scores the patient. And this has to be fast. The clinician is waiting, so your latency budget is tight. Step four: your service returns a card, and the EHR displays it. Below is a mockup of what that can look like: a short message, where the score came from, a link to explain it, and actions — open an order set, or dismiss with a reason. That “dismiss with a reason” button is your evaluation data. Every override tells you something about your model or your alert design. Remember question five from the Build Check: the override rate is your guardrail metric. You’ll build this full flow in Track C.
