CHEAT SHEET

Six patterns at a glance

Pattern Trigger Scope Clinical AI use Watch out
HL7 v2 feed Event: admit, result Every event, streaming Real-time features Formats vary by site
FHIR query Your request One or a few patients On-demand features Rate limits, paging
Bulk $export Schedule Whole population Training, drift monitoring Asynchronous, not real-time
SMART app A person opens it Patient in context Clinician and patient apps App registration, security review
CDS Hooks Workflow moment Current patient Alerts and advice Latency budget, alert fatigue
C-CDA document Transition of care One snapshot Notes and summaries as input Static, patient matching
Here’s the whole lecture on one slide. Screenshot it — it’s also in the resources as a PDF. HL7 v2 feed: triggered by events like admissions and results, streaming for every patient. In clinical AI, that’s your source of real-time features. Watch out: the format varies from site to site, so every new hospital means new mapping work. FHIR query: triggered by your request, for one or a few patients. Great for pulling features on demand. Watch out for rate limits and paging on large result sets. Bulk export: runs on a schedule, for the whole population. That’s your training data and your drift monitoring. It runs asynchronously — you start the export and collect the files later — so it’s never real-time. SMART app: triggered when a person opens it, with a patient in context. That’s how you build clinician-facing or patient-facing apps. Expect app registration and a security review with each EHR. CDS Hooks: triggered at a moment in the workflow — opening a chart, signing an order. That’s where AI alerts and advice live. Watch out: you have a tight latency budget, and a badly tuned alert creates alert fatigue fast. And C-CDA documents: a snapshot sent at a transition of care, like a discharge. Useful as input for NLP. But it’s static — it doesn’t update — and matching it to the right patient takes care.
