Teal: the why and who — from your Build Decision Record. Dark: the how — written with the interface team.

Here’s the IDD. Eight sections. One: purpose and workflow — the decision, the person, the moment, and a simple workflow diagram. Two: the pattern, the standard and security — exactly what you chose in lecture 2, made precise. Three: data scope and mapping — your must, later and out lists, plus the field mapping. Four: identity and matching. Five: errors and monitoring. Six: constraints and assumptions. Seven: success metrics — one for each of the three layers. Eight: owners and sign-off. Notice the colors. The teal sections — purpose, metrics, owners — you mostly already have from your Build Decision Record. Copy them over. The dark sections are the how, and you write those together with the interface team. Keep it short. A good IDD for one interface is a few pages, not fifty. If nobody reads it, it’s not a source of truth. Let’s look at the sections where teams most often go wrong.
